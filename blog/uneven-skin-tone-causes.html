SKIN CONCERNS

What Causes Uneven Skin Tone?

Uneven-looking skin tone can have several causes. Understanding common contributing factors can help you build a gentle, realistic skincare routine without expecting overnight changes.

By Anarat Naturals Editorial Team · November 11, 2026 · General skincare education

Uneven skin tone is a broad term people use when some areas of the complexion look darker, lighter or less uniform than surrounding skin. It can appear gradually or after a blemish, irritation or period of increased sun exposure. The reason is not always the same for everyone, so it helps to look at the context rather than relying on one product or ingredient.

1. Sun exposure

Ultraviolet (UV) exposure can contribute to changes in skin pigmentation. Areas that receive frequent daylight exposure may develop a more uneven appearance over time. Daily broad-spectrum sunscreen, used according to its label directions and reapplied as directed, is an important part of a routine focused on maintaining an even-looking complexion.

2. Marks left after blemishes

After spots or other inflammation settle, some people notice flat areas of discolouration where the blemish was. These are often called post-inflammatory marks. They are different from an active spot and may take time to fade. Avoid picking or squeezing blemishes, as this can increase irritation and the chance of marks.

3. Skin irritation and harsh routines

Scrubbing, over-exfoliating, using too many new products at once or continuing a product that causes irritation can make skin uncomfortable. In some people, inflammation may be followed by changes in pigmentation. A simpler routine and careful product introduction can help reduce avoidable irritation.

4. Hormonal and individual factors

Hormonal changes, genetics, age and some medications or health conditions can influence pigmentation. Melasma, for example, is a specific pigmentation condition that can be influenced by hormones and sunlight. A general cosmetic routine cannot diagnose or treat every cause.

5. Dryness and a dull appearance

Dehydration or a disrupted skin barrier can make the surface look dull or less uniform, even when the underlying issue is not pigmentation. A moisturiser suited to your skin and gentle cleansing can support a more comfortable, hydrated appearance.

Simple habits to support your routine

  • Use a gentle cleanser and avoid aggressive scrubbing.
  • Apply a moisturiser that suits your skin and feels comfortable.
  • Use broad-spectrum sunscreen during the day and follow its reapplication directions.
  • Introduce one new skincare product at a time and patch test as directed.
  • Avoid picking at spots or repeatedly changing your routine.

A helpful reminder: skincare results vary. Cosmetic products are not medical treatments and cannot diagnose or treat pigmentation conditions. Follow product directions and seek advice from a qualified dermatologist for persistent or concerning skin changes.

Frequently asked questions

Does uneven skin tone always mean hyperpigmentation?

No. Uneven-looking tone can describe several different appearances, including post-blemish marks, sun-related pigmentation, redness or dullness. A dermatologist can help identify the cause when it is persistent or changing.

Can skincare improve the appearance of uneven skin tone?

Some cosmetic products are formulated to improve the appearance of uneven-looking tone, but results vary by formula and individual. Choose products with clear directions and realistic claims.

When should I see a dermatologist?

Seek professional advice if pigmentation appears suddenly, changes quickly, is widespread, is associated with other symptoms, or does not improve. A dermatologist can assess the cause and discuss appropriate options.
