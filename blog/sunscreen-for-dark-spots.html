SUN PROTECTION

Sunscreen for Dark Spots: Why Daily Sun Protection Matters

A skincare routine aimed at a more even-looking complexion should not overlook sun protection. Learn what dermatologists recommend and how to make sunscreen a consistent daily habit.

By Anarat Naturals Editorial Team · November 11, 2026 · General skincare education

Why sun exposure matters

Sun exposure can contribute to the development or darkening of visible pigmentation. For people dealing with dark spots or melasma, ultraviolet radiation and, in some cases, visible light can make discoloration more noticeable. Skincare products alone cannot replace practical sun-protection habits.

What to look for in a sunscreen

The American Academy of Dermatology recommends choosing a sunscreen that offers broad-spectrum protection, SPF 30 or higher and water resistance. Broad-spectrum means the product protects against both UVA and UVB rays. Choose a texture you are comfortable applying generously and consistently.

How to apply sunscreen

Apply sunscreen to exposed skin before going outdoors and follow the label directions. Reapply at least every two hours when outdoors, and after swimming, sweating or towel-drying as directed. Use shade, protective clothing and a wide-brimmed hat as additional measures.

What about visible light and tinted sunscreen?

Visible light can worsen pigmentation for some people, particularly those with darker skin tones. The AAD notes that tinted sunscreens containing iron oxides can offer additional protection against visible light. This is an option to discuss with a dermatologist if pigmentation is a concern.

How sunscreen fits with a skincare routine

A simple morning routine can include gentle cleansing, moisturiser if needed and sunscreen as the final skincare step before makeup. If you use a cosmetic cream containing ingredients such as niacinamide or alpha arbutin, use it according to its label and do not treat it as a substitute for sunscreen.

When to seek professional advice

Dark patches can have different causes, and not every mark is ordinary pigmentation. If spots are changing, spreading, painful, itchy or not improving, consult a dermatologist. Avoid unlabelled lightening products or products promising rapid changes to natural skin colour.

Frequently asked questions

Does sunscreen help prevent dark spots from getting darker?

Sun protection can help prevent sun-related darkening and is an important part of care for many pigmentation concerns.

What SPF should I choose?

Look for broad-spectrum SPF 30 or higher and water resistance, as recommended by the American Academy of Dermatology.

How often should I reapply sunscreen?

When outdoors, reapply at least every two hours and after swimming, sweating or towel-drying, following the product label.
